Atom: the periodic table, computed

A free periodic table that calculates every element from the equations and lets you build, predict, and work through the physics of any atom. No accounts, no servers, nothing sent anywhere. It works in any browser, offline after the first visit, in English and Spanish.

For students

Start anywhere. Click an element. Scroll to fall into it: its electron cloud, its light, the empty space, its nucleus, a proton, and the quarks you can try to pull out.

The labs. Build an atom or a molecule. Predict, and the questions you miss come back later until you get them right. Work through The math of any atom, ten steps from the outside in. Hear an element's light. Run a fusion reaction or build a battery.

Your progress stays on your device, in your browser. On a shared computer, press Save my progress before you leave and Bring progress back next time.

Reading help. Under Learning settings, Simpler rewrites the explanations in plainer words, Term help explains the science words when you tap them, and Listen reads a passage aloud.

For teachers

Assign anything with a link. Every lab, element, and step has its own address, like #lab=build&z=26. Paste it into your LMS or a slide. ?lang=es opens it in Spanish.

Five lesson paths of about 45 minutes, each ending in an exit check of six fixed questions, the same for the whole class. They map to NGSS HS-PS1-1, HS-PS1-8, HS-PS4-3, HS-ESS1-3, and AP Chemistry units 1 and 2.

Collect work without accounts. Students copy a one-line turn-in code from the You tab and paste it into your form. It holds the date, their counts, their exit-check scores, and no name. Paste a class's codes into Read the codes for a table. Worksheets print with the answer key on the last page.

The teacher guide at /atom/teachers.html has a first lesson minute by minute, the standards map, and a short report form for pilots.

For families

It is free and open source (MIT License), made by a professor at California State University, Dominguez Hills. It costs nothing and sells nothing.

It is private. Atom has no accounts and makes no network requests after it loads. Nothing a student does leaves the device. There is no chat, no sharing, and no advertising.

It works on what you have: a phone, a tablet, a Chromebook, or a desktop, in any modern browser, and it keeps working without Wi-Fi after the first visit. Desktop apps for Mac, Windows, and Linux are on the site.

In Spanish, too. The language menu is in the top bar of every page. Term help shows the English word beside the Spanish one, so a student can learn the science in both languages at once.
